### 1. Purpose

This document defines the plan for conducting the clinical evaluation of ClarityCheck v1.0 in accordance with MDR 2017/745 Article 61, Annex XIV Part A, and MEDDEV 2.7/1 revision 4. It defines the scope, methodology, literature search strategy, appraisal criteria, and planned outputs of the clinical evaluation.

The output of this plan is **CE-003 Clinical Evaluation Report (CER)**, which will be submitted as part of the technical documentation for conformity assessment.

---

### 2. Scope of Clinical Evaluation

The clinical evaluation shall address:

- The clinical validity and performance of each instrument in the v1.0 battery
- The clinical benefit of the combined battery for the stated intended purpose
- The safety profile of the device in the intended use context
- The state of the art for ADHD screening and comorbidity assessment in adults
- The benefit-risk profile of ClarityCheck v1.0 relative to available alternatives

The evaluation is scoped to ClarityCheck v1.0 as defined in DC-002. Instruments excluded from v1.0 (ASRS Part B, ISI) are out of scope.

---

### 3. Clinical Background

#### 3.1 Condition

Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterised by persistent inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity. In adults, ADHD is associated with significant occupational, social, and psychological impairment.

Key epidemiological facts relevant to intended purpose:

- Estimated adult ADHD prevalence: 2.5–4% globally (Fayyad et al., 2017)
- Adult ADHD is substantially underdiagnosed — mean delay from symptom onset to diagnosis exceeds 10 years in many health systems
- Comorbid depression is present in approximately 50% of adults with ADHD
- Comorbid anxiety disorders are present in approximately 50% of adults with ADHD
- Comorbid sleep disorders are present in approximately 30–50% (noted as v1.1 scope per DDR-001)

#### 3.2 State of the Art

The clinical evaluation shall review:

- Current clinical guidelines for adult ADHD assessment (NICE NG87, European Consensus Statement, CHADD guidelines)
- Existing validated screening tools for adult ADHD (ASRS v1.1, ASRS-5, Conners CAARS, Brown ADD Scales)
- Existing digital self-assessment tools in this domain
- Relevant regulatory guidance for SaMD in the mental health domain

#### 3.3 Unmet Need

Self-administered screening tools that support earlier help-seeking in undiagnosed adults represent a recognised gap. Primary care access is limited in many health systems; a validated, accessible screening battery that prepares users for clinical consultation has a plausible clinical benefit pathway.

### 4. Clinical Evaluation Methodology

In accordance with MEDDEV 2.7/1 rev 4, the clinical evaluation shall be conducted via **literature-based clinical evaluation** (Stage 1 + Stage 2 route). No clinical investigation is planned for v1.0, as the device is based entirely on pre-existing validated instruments with substantial published evidence bases.

The evaluation shall follow four stages:

| Stage | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1 | Define scope and intended purpose |
| 2 | Identify pertinent data via literature search |
| 3 | Appraise and analyse identified data |
| 4 | Generate Clinical Evaluation Report (CE-003) |

---

### 5. Literature Search Strategy

A systematic literature search shall be conducted for each instrument in the v1.0 battery. Searches shall be performed in PubMed/MEDLINE and, where relevant, PsycINFO and Cochrane Library.

#### 5.1 ASRS v1.1 Part A — Search Parameters

**Objectives:**
- Establish original development and validation evidence
- Identify sensitivity and specificity data across populations
- Identify any known limitations or failure modes relevant to intended use

**Search terms:**
- "Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale" AND "validation"
- "ASRS" AND "sensitivity" AND "specificity"
- "ASRS" AND "adult" AND "screening"
- "Kessler" AND "ADHD" AND "self-report"

**Key anticipated sources:**
- Kessler et al. (2005) — original validation, *Psychological Medicine* 35(2):245–256
- Kessler et al. (2007) — population prevalence study using ASRS
- Adler et al. (2006) — further validation data
- Additional independent validation studies across European, Asian, and general populations

**Inclusion criteria:**
- Studies using ASRS v1.1 or Part A screener
- Adult populations (18+)
- Peer-reviewed, published in English, German, French, or Italian
- Validation, psychometric, or diagnostic accuracy study design

**Exclusion criteria:**
- Paediatric or adolescent populations
- Studies using ASRS-5 (revised version) without v1.1 comparison
- Non-peer-reviewed sources

---

#### 5.2 PHQ-9 — Search Parameters

**Objectives:**
- Establish development and validation evidence for depression screening
- Identify sensitivity, specificity, and cut-point performance data
- Identify evidence for use in populations with ADHD comorbidity

**Search terms:**
- "PHQ-9" AND "validation"
- "Patient Health Questionnaire" AND "sensitivity" AND "specificity"
- "PHQ-9" AND "primary care" AND "depression screening"
- "PHQ-9" AND "ADHD" OR "attention deficit"

**Key anticipated sources:**
- Kroenke et al. (2001) — original validation, *Journal of General Internal Medicine*
- Spitzer et al. (1999) — PHQ development paper
- Löwe et al. (2004) — systematic review of PHQ-9 diagnostic accuracy
- Manea et al. (2012) — meta-analysis of PHQ-9 optimal cut-point

**Inclusion/exclusion criteria:** As per ASRS above.

**Objectives:**
- Establish validation evidence for anxiety screening
- Identify sensitivity, specificity, and cut-point performance
- Identify evidence for use alongside PHQ-9 in combined screening

**Search terms:**
- "GAD-7" AND "validation"

- "GAD-7" AND "PHQ-9" AND "comorbidity"

**Key anticipated sources:**

#### 5.4 Combined Battery and Digital Delivery — Search Parameters

**Objectives:**
- Identify evidence for combined ADHD + comorbidity screening batteries

- Identify state-of-the-art digital mental health screening tools for comparison
- Identify any adverse outcomes or safety signals associated with self-administered mental health screening

**Search terms:**
- "ADHD" AND "depression" AND "anxiety" AND "comorbidity" AND "screening"
- "digital" AND "ADHD screening" AND "adult"
- "self-administered" AND "mental health screening" AND "safety"
- "online" AND "PHQ-9" AND "GAD-7" AND "validation"

### 6. Data Appraisal Criteria

Each identified study shall be appraised using the following criteria, consistent with MEDDEV 2.7/1 rev 4 Appendix A1:

| Criterion | Description |
|---|---|
| Relevance | Does the study address the instrument as used in ClarityCheck v1.0 (adult population, self-administered, English language)? |
| Methodological quality | Is the study design appropriate? Is the reference standard valid? Is sample size adequate? |
| Study population | Is the population comparable to the intended user population of ClarityCheck v1.0? |
| Outcome measures | Are sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and/or normative score data reported? |
| Applicability | Can findings be applied to the ClarityCheck v1.0 use context? |

Each study shall be assigned a quality weighting:

| Weight | Description |
|---|---|
| High | Prospective validation study, adequate sample, appropriate reference standard |
| Moderate | Retrospective or secondary analysis, reasonable methodology |
| Low | Case series, opinion, or indirect evidence |

### 7. Clinical Performance Parameters

The CER shall report the following performance parameters for each instrument:

| Parameter | Source |
|---|---|
| Sensitivity | Validation literature |
| Specificity | Validation literature |
| Positive predictive value | Validation literature or calculation |
| Negative predictive value | Validation literature or calculation |
| Internal consistency (Cronbach's α) | Psychometric literature |
| Test-retest reliability | Psychometric literature |
| Cut-point justification | Original validation and guideline alignment |

These parameters shall be used in RM-004 to quantify the clinical basis for residual risk acceptability.

---

### 8. Safety Data Requirements

The CER shall address:

- Known adverse effects of self-administered mental health screening (distress, false reassurance, stigma)
- Safeguarding evidence for PHQ-9 item 9 crisis response in digital tools
- Evidence base for crisis resource signposting effectiveness
- Any reported serious adverse events associated with self-administered ADHD or mental health screening tools in the literature

---

### 9. Equivalence Assessment

ClarityCheck v1.0 is not claimed to be equivalent to any predicate device. No equivalence route is pursued. The clinical evaluation is based entirely on the intrinsic clinical validity of the validated instruments used and their established evidence bases.

This decision shall be documented in CE-003 with rationale.

### 11. Clinical Evaluator Qualifications

The clinical evaluation will be conducted by the solo developer acting as PRRC. This represents a known limitation for a Class IIa device under MDR Article 61.

Mitigation:

- All conclusions shall be anchored to peer-reviewed published evidence rather than clinical judgement
- The evaluation methodology follows MEDDEV 2.7/1 rev 4 explicitly and systematically
- No proprietary clinical data or novel clinical claims are made — the evaluation relies entirely on the published validation record of established instruments
- A qualified clinician reviewer (GP, psychiatrist, or psychologist) shall be sought to review CE-003 prior to submission, and this review shall be documented

**Action:** Identify and engage a clinical reviewer before CE-003 is finalised. Document their qualifications and review in CE-003.

### 12. Update and Maintenance

The clinical evaluation is a living process. CE-003 shall be updated:

- At each major version release (v1.1, v2.0)
- When post-market surveillance data (PM-001) identifies new safety signals
- When significant new literature is published that materially affects the benefit-risk conclusion
- At minimum every 12 months, per MDR Article 61(11) for Class IIa devices

---

### 13. Relationship to Risk Management

The clinical evaluation directly supports RM-004 Residual Risk Evaluation:

- Sensitivity and specificity data quantify the magnitude of false negative and false positive risks (H1–H4)
- Evidence for safeguarding approaches supports the ALARP justification for H5
- Overall benefit-risk conclusion in CE-003 is the primary input to the overall residual risk acceptance decision in RM-004
