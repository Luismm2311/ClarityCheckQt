# ClarityCheck
## Instructions for Use
### Version 1.0

---

## Important — Please Read Before You Start

ClarityCheck is a screening tool. It is **not** a diagnostic tool. It cannot tell you whether you have ADHD or any other condition. Only a qualified healthcare professional can do that.

If you are in crisis or are having thoughts of harming yourself, **please do not use this app right now.** Contact a crisis service or go to your nearest emergency department.

- **UK:** Samaritans — 116 123 (free, 24/7)
- **US:** 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline — call or text 988
- **EU:** Find your national crisis line at findahelpline.com

---

## 1. What is ClarityCheck?

ClarityCheck is a free, self-administered app that helps adults who think they might have ADHD prepare for a conversation with their doctor or another healthcare professional.

It asks you a series of questions about your attention, mood, and anxiety. When you finish, it gives you a score for each set of questions and explains what your scores might mean.

**ClarityCheck is designed to:**
- Help you understand your symptoms better
- Give you useful information to share with a healthcare professional
- Support you in taking the first step towards getting help

**ClarityCheck is not designed to:**
- Diagnose ADHD, depression, anxiety, or any other condition
- Replace assessment by a doctor, psychiatrist, or psychologist
- Tell you whether or not you have ADHD
- Recommend any medication or treatment

---

## 2. Who is ClarityCheck For?

ClarityCheck is for **adults aged 18 and over** who want to find out more about their attention and mental health symptoms.

**Do not use ClarityCheck if you are under 18.** The questions in this app were developed and tested for adults. The scores may not be meaningful for people under 18. If you are under 18 and have concerns about ADHD, please speak to your doctor or a parent or guardian. Your doctor can refer you to the right service for your age group.

ClarityCheck is suitable for you if:
- You are 18 or over
- You are concerned that you may have ADHD
- You have not yet spoken to a doctor about these concerns, or you want to prepare for that conversation
- You are able to answer questions about how you have been feeling over the past two weeks to six months

---

## 3. What ClarityCheck Does Not Cover

ClarityCheck v1.0 assesses three areas: ADHD symptoms, depression, and anxiety.

**It does not assess:**
- Sleep problems (a future version of ClarityCheck will include sleep assessment)
- Other mental health conditions such as bipolar disorder, autism, or PTSD
- Physical health conditions
- The severity of your ADHD symptoms in detail — this version uses a short 6-question ADHD screener only

If you have concerns about any of these areas, please speak to your doctor.

## 4. The Questions in ClarityCheck

ClarityCheck contains three sets of questions. You will answer all three in order.

### Part 1 — ADHD Symptoms
**Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS v1.1 Screener)**
6 questions about your attention and concentration over the past 6 months.

Developed by the World Health Organization and Harvard Medical School.
© New York University and the President and Fellows of Harvard College. All rights reserved.
*Kessler et al. (2005). Psychological Medicine, 35(2), 245–256.*

### Part 2 — Depression Symptoms
**Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9)**
9 questions about your mood over the past 2 weeks.

© 1999 Pfizer Inc. All rights reserved. Reproduced with permission. No permission required to reproduce, translate, display, or distribute.
*Kroenke et al. (2001). Journal of General Internal Medicine, 16(9), 606–613.*

### Part 3 — Anxiety Symptoms
**Generalized Anxiety Disorder Scale (GAD-7)**
7 questions about your anxiety over the past 2 weeks.

© 1999 Pfizer Inc. All rights reserved. Reproduced with permission. No permission required to reproduce, translate, display, or distribute.
*Spitzer et al. (2006). Archives of Internal Medicine, 166(10), 1092–1097.*

**Total: 22 questions. Most people complete ClarityCheck in 5–10 minutes.**

## 5. Before You Start

Take a moment to check the following:

**You are 18 or over.**
These questions were developed for adults. The scores are not valid for people under 18.

**You are in a private, comfortable place.**
Some questions ask about sensitive topics including mood, anxiety, and thoughts of self-harm. It is best to complete ClarityCheck somewhere you feel safe and are not rushed.

**You have 10–15 minutes without interruption.**
Try not to stop halfway through. Completing all questions gives you the most useful results.

**You know that ClarityCheck is not a diagnosis.**
Whatever your score, you will need to speak to a healthcare professional to find out whether you have ADHD or any other condition.

**You are not in crisis.**
If you are having thoughts of harming yourself, please contact a crisis service before using this app (see Section 1).

---

## 6. How to Use ClarityCheck

**Step 1 — Read the welcome screen**
The app will show you a brief introduction and ask you to confirm that you are 18 or over and that you understand ClarityCheck is a screening tool, not a diagnostic tool.

**Step 2 — Answer the questions**
Answer each question as honestly as you can. Think about how you have actually been feeling, not how you would like to feel or think you should feel.

- For ADHD questions: think about the **past 6 months**
- For depression and anxiety questions: think about the **past 2 weeks**

You can change your answer before moving to the next question. You cannot go back to a previous section once you have moved on.

There are no right or wrong answers. The most useful answers are the most honest ones.

**Step 3 — Complete all questions**
You must answer every question to receive your results. The app will not allow you to skip questions.

**Step 4 — View your results**
When you have answered all 22 questions, the app will show your scores and explain what they may mean. Read the full results screen carefully before sharing your results with anyone.

**Step 5 — Share with your doctor (optional)**
You can save or print your results to share with a healthcare professional. See Section 9 for important advice about sharing your results.

---

## 7. Understanding Your Results

Your results will show a score for each of the three sections. Here is what the scores mean.

### ADHD Screener (ASRS Part A) — Score 0 to 6

| Result | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Positive screen** (4 or more symptoms highly consistent) | Your answers suggest symptoms that are highly consistent with ADHD in adults. This does not mean you have ADHD. It means your symptoms are worth discussing with a doctor. |
| **Negative screen** (fewer than 4 symptoms) | Your answers did not reach the threshold for a positive ADHD screen. This does not rule out ADHD. If you still have concerns, speak to your doctor. |

**Important:** The ASRS screener identifies approximately 2 in 3 adults with ADHD. Approximately 1 in 3 adults with ADHD will score below the threshold. A negative result does not mean you do not have ADHD.

### Depression (PHQ-9) — Score 0 to 27

| Score | Category | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 | Minimal or none | Few or no symptoms of depression at this time |
| 5–9 | Mild | Some symptoms that may be worth monitoring |
| 10–14 | Moderate | Symptoms that are worth discussing with your doctor |
| 15–19 | Moderately severe | Symptoms that suggest you should speak to your doctor soon |
| 20–27 | Severe | Symptoms that suggest you should speak to your doctor as soon as possible |

A score of 10 or above does not mean you have depression. It means your symptoms are significant enough to warrant a clinical assessment.

### Anxiety (GAD-7) — Score 0 to 21

| Score | Category | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 | Minimal or none | Few or no symptoms of anxiety at this time |
| 5–9 | Mild | Some symptoms that may be worth monitoring |
| 10–14 | Moderate | Symptoms that are worth discussing with your doctor |
| 15–21 | Severe | Symptoms that suggest you should speak to your doctor soon |

A score of 10 or above does not mean you have an anxiety disorder. It means your symptoms are significant enough to warrant a clinical assessment.

## 8. Important Limitations

**A positive result is not a diagnosis.**
Scoring positive on the ADHD screener, or scoring in the moderate or severe range on the PHQ-9 or GAD-7, does not mean you have ADHD, depression, or an anxiety disorder. Many conditions can cause similar symptoms. Only a qualified clinician can make a diagnosis.

**A negative result does not rule anything out.**
No screening tool is 100% accurate. If you score below the threshold on any section but still have significant concerns, please speak to your doctor.

**This app does not assess all possible conditions.**
ClarityCheck screens for three specific areas only. It does not assess other conditions that can cause similar symptoms, including autism, bipolar disorder, sleep disorders, thyroid problems, and others.

**Your score may vary on different days.**
Screening questionnaires reflect how you have been feeling over a specific period. Your score may be different if you repeat the assessment at another time.

**ClarityCheck does not replace clinical assessment.**
A full ADHD assessment involves a detailed clinical interview, history-taking, and often input from people who knew you as a child. ClarityCheck is a first step, not a substitute for that process.

## 9. Sharing Your Results

You can save or print your results to share with a healthcare professional. This can be helpful when you first speak to your doctor about your concerns.

**Please read the following before sharing your results:**

Your results are a **self-reported screening output**. They are not a clinical assessment, a medical record, or a diagnosis. The printed results document will include a statement to this effect.

**Share your results with:**
- Your GP or family doctor
- A psychiatrist or psychologist
- A mental health practitioner

**Do not share your results with:**
- Employers or prospective employers
- Insurance companies
- Schools or educational institutions
- Any person or organisation who might use them to make decisions about you

Sharing uncontextualised screening scores with employers or insurers could have unintended consequences. A screening score is not a medical record and should not be treated as one.

**ClarityCheck does not store your results.** When you close the app or your browser tab, your results are permanently deleted. If you want to keep your results, save or print them before closing the app.

---

## 10. About ADHD Medication

**ClarityCheck does not recommend any medication.**

ADHD medication — including stimulant medications such as methylphenidate and amphetamines — requires a formal clinical assessment and a prescription from a qualified doctor. It must not be obtained or used without professional supervision.

A positive result on the ClarityCheck ADHD screener is not a basis for self-medicating. If you are considering ADHD medication, speak to your doctor first.

## 11. Technical Requirements

ClarityCheck runs in a web browser on most modern devices.

**Internet connection:** Required to load the app. No data is sent to any server during your assessment.
**Screen size:** Optimised for screens 320px wide and above

**Accessibility:** ClarityCheck aims to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA accessibility standards. If you experience any difficulty using the app, please contact us (see Section 13).

## 12. Privacy

ClarityCheck v1.0 does not collect, store, or transmit any personal data.

- Your answers are processed entirely on your own device
- No information is sent to any server
- No account or login is required
- Your results are deleted when you close the app or browser tab
